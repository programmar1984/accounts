1. Vehicle Purchase Cost (仕入高)

When you buy a vehicle at auction for resale/export, all costs incurred to acquire the inventory and make it ready for sale are normally included in inventory (棚卸資産).

Example:

Item	Accounting Treatment
Auction hammer price	Purchase (仕入)
Auction fee (落札料)	Purchase (仕入)
Auction membership fee directly related to purchase	Purchase (仕入)
Recycling fee (リサイクル預託金)	Asset (not expense)
Consumption tax (if deductible)	Input tax credit
Road tax reimbursement (自動車税相当額)	Usually receivable/payable adjustment, not vehicle cost
Important Note About Recycling Fee

Many foreign exporters mistakenly include the recycling fee in vehicle cost.

In Japan, the recycling fee is generally treated as a deposit-type asset (預託金) rather than an expense or inventory cost.

Example:

Vehicle Purchase     500,000
Auction Fee           15,000
Recycle Fee           10,000

Journal:

Inventory (Cars)      515,000
Recycling Deposit      10,000
    Accounts Payable        525,000

When the vehicle is exported, there is a separate treatment for recovering or disposing of the recycling deposit.

2. Inland Transportation to Port

Example:

Auction Yard → Yokohama Port

Cost:

30,000 JPY

There are two possible treatments.

Method A (Recommended for exporters)

Capitalize into Inventory

Inventory (Cars)      30,000
    Accounts Payable      30,000

Reason:

This cost was incurred to bring the vehicle into a saleable/exportable condition.

This is often preferred because it reflects the true profit per vehicle.

Method B

Expense immediately

Freight Expense (荷造運賃) 30,000
    Accounts Payable          30,000

Many small exporters use this method because it is simpler.

3. Shipping Charges (Ocean Freight)

Example:

Ocean Freight      120,000

Again, there are two acceptable approaches.

If you want accurate vehicle profitability

Include in inventory cost:

Inventory (Cars)   120,000
    Accounts Payable    120,000

Then when the vehicle is sold:

Cost of Goods Sold
    Inventory

The freight becomes part of COGS automatically.

If using simpler bookkeeping
Export Freight Expense
    Accounts Payable

This is common among smaller exporters.

4. Should Shipping Companies Have Purchase Orders?

From an accounting standpoint:

No, a Purchase Order (PO) is not legally required.

What matters is:

Invoice (請求書)
Contract/Booking confirmation
Payment record

However, from a business management perspective, many exporters create a PO for:

Inland transport
Port handling
Shipping line charges
Container stuffing

because they want to track costs per vehicle.

Example:

Vehicle ABC123

Purchase:
  Auction Price      500,000
  Auction Fee         15,000

Logistics:
  Inland Transport    30,000
  Ocean Freight      120,000
  Port Charges        20,000

Whether you call these "Purchase Orders" or "Service Orders" depends on your ERP/accounting system.

In systems such as SAP S/4HANA, freight vendors are often processed through service purchase orders. In simpler systems such as freee or Money Forward Cloud, many businesses simply record the vendor invoice without a formal PO.

How Most Japanese Used-Car Exporters Do It

In practice, many exporters maintain a vehicle-cost sheet (車両原価表) for each unit:

Auction Price         500,000
Auction Fee            15,000
Inland Transport       30,000
Port Charges           20,000
Ocean Freight         120,000
--------------------------------
Total Landed Cost     685,000

Then, when the vehicle is sold:

Sales Price           850,000
Cost of Goods Sold    685,000
Gross Profit          165,000

This approach gives the clearest picture of profitability and is usually what a Zeirishi would recommend if you're exporting vehicles regularly and want accurate margins by vehicle.
